# Agent foundation: working proposals

This document records an organizational approach discussed in the initial conversation. It is not a finalized system prompt.

## Context layers

1. **Guiding principles:** purpose, voice, relationship to the person, boundaries, and spiritual orientation.
2. **Knowledge library:** sourced teachings, the creator's interpretations, and relevant passages retrieved when useful.
3. **Exercise library:** activities, prompts, suitable situations, and structured UI interactions.
4. **Example conversations:** desired and undesired responses, with explanations, used to refine and evaluate behavior.

Begin with instructions, curated knowledge, tools, and examples. Fine-tuning is not an established requirement. Keep core instructions compact; avoid assuming the entire library must be included in every conversation.

## Proposed behavioral commitments

These need the creator's review before becoming agent instructions:

- Be warm, honest, and direct; support reflection without shame.
- Explore compulsive patterns without assigning an addiction label or assuming everyone is addicted.
- Respect personal agency and spiritual beliefs; do not claim spiritual authority or promise awakening.
- Present Jungian interpretations as possibilities for reflection, not diagnoses or established facts about the person.
- Encourage appropriate human connection, including peers, sponsors, and qualified professionals.
- Do not present the companion as a replacement for clinical care or a human recovery sponsor.
- Recognize urgent distress and dangerous withdrawal as situations requiring appropriate human help.
- Preserve the original Step Nine concern about harm when discussing amends.

## Exercises and UI

The agent could select and personalize curated components rather than inventing arbitrary interfaces. An exercise definition could include its purpose, related steps and principles, prompts, interaction type, relevant cautions, and a reflection or next action.

Illustrative exercise discussed: identify what a person is trying to control, distinguish areas of influence, reflect on acceptance, and choose one concrete action. This example is not yet part of an approved exercise library.

Additional UI ideas supplied by the creator alongside Steps 4–7:

- A moral inventory capturing perceived wrongs, resentments, and reflection on one's own part.
- A way to choose a buddy for sharing the inventory; whether Step Five requires a human rather than AI is unresolved.
- A written inventory of character defects, supporting readiness, asking a higher power for help, and ongoing awareness.

These are concept notes, not implemented features. Actual inventory sharing requires explicit consent. Reflection on one's own part must not become an assumption that someone is responsible for abuse or other harm done to them.

Additional ideas supplied by the creator alongside Steps 8–10:

- An amends list recording people one has harmed and willingness to repair that harm.
- Consideration of whether contact would cause further harm, including potentially detrimental contact with a former partner.
- A daily mini inventory for ongoing maintenance, prompt acknowledgment, and appropriate amends.
- An everyday example of apologizing by email after behaving poorly in a meeting.

The email example is not authorization for automated outreach. Any future external messaging feature needs a separate design for explicit authorization and review.

Additional ideas supplied by the creator alongside Steps 11–12:

- Daily meditation as a key practice, with possible durations of one, two, five, or thirty minutes.
- Prayer centered on guidance and strength, using a form the person finds useful.
- Reflection on insights that may arise through meditation.
- An exercise for passing the practice on to another person, potentially through sharing or service.

Specific interfaces remain to be designed. The agent should not claim to know God's will, promise answers through meditation, or certify spiritual awakening. Sharing should be voluntary and respect the recipient's willingness.

## Personal context and privacy

Personal goals, reflections, preferred spiritual language, and commitments are distinct from the shared knowledge library. Design consent, sharing, retention, viewing, and deletion explicitly. Do not assume a ChatGPT app receives the person's full ChatGPT history or memory.
