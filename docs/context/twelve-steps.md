# Twelve Steps: source and adaptation

## Original AA Steps

The following wording was supplied by the creator as the AA Twelve Steps. It is retained as source context, separately from any Soulfire adaptation. An authoritative source and reuse requirements should be checked before public publication.

1. We admitted we were powerless over alcohol — that our lives had become unmanageable.
2. Came to believe that a Power greater than ourselves could restore us to sanity.
3. Made a decision to turn our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understood Him.
4. Made a searching and fearless moral inventory of ourselves.
5. Admitted to God, to ourselves, and to another human being the exact nature of our wrongs.
6. Were entirely ready to have God remove all these defects of character.
7. Humbly asked Him to remove our shortcomings.
8. Made a list of all persons we had harmed, and became willing to make amends to them all.
9. Made direct amends to such people wherever possible, except when to do so would injure them or others.
10. Continued to take personal inventory and when we were wrong promptly admitted it.
11. Sought through prayer and meditation to improve our conscious contact with God as we understood Him, praying only for knowledge of His will for us and the power to carry that out.
12. Having had a spiritual awakening as the result of these Steps, we tried to carry this message to alcoholics, and to practice these principles in all our affairs.

## Soulfire adaptation

The following interpretations were supplied by the creator. All twelve steps now have an initial interpretation. They capture the developing spiritual framework, not finalized agent instructions or clinical claims. Keep the creator's explanations distinct from implementation proposals.

### Step 1: Powerlessness and resistance to the present moment

Creator's wording:

> We admitted we are powerless over literally anything. We are powerless over what's being manifested in the present moment, and a lot of times we resist it. We resist it to a point where it makes our lives unmanageable.

The creator further explains that we have little control over how the present moment manifests, and that this brings a lot of unhappiness. This expands the original Step One beyond alcohol to the experience of resisting what is happening in life.

### Step 2: Belief in a higher power

Creator's wording:

> We've come to believe that through spirituality, through finding a power greater than ourselves, we can be restored to sanity.

In the creator's explanation, acknowledging powerlessness is followed by the belief that a higher power can restore us to sanity.

### Step 3: Turning things over

Creator's wording:

> Made a decision to turn our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understood Him.

> So we found a higher power, and now we turn things over. Every conflict, every problem we have, everything that gets complicated, we turn it over to a higher power.

The emphasis is on applying this surrender to everyday conflicts and complications.

### Step 4: Moral inventory and resentments

Creator's wording:

> We made searching and fearless moral inventory of ourselves.

> We capture everything that we did quote-unquote wrong, and all of our resentments. In our resentments we find, like what is our part in the resentment?

The creator considers this important for people with and without addiction. Their view is that accumulated resentments can reinforce a victim mentality, and that examining one's own part in each resentment can change that experience profoundly. Preserve this as the creator's philosophy, rather than an established claim about every person's circumstances.

### Step 5: Sharing the inventory

Creator's wording:

> Admit to God, to ourselves, and to another human being the exact nature of our wrongs.

The person reads or shares their inventory with someone else, such as a sponsor. The creator initially considers sharing with the AI, then leaves open whether this step must involve another human being.

UI idea from the creator: allow the person to choose a buddy. Whether the product supports buddy selection alone, preparation for an outside conversation, or actual sharing is undecided.

### Step 6: Identifying character defects and becoming ready

Creator's wording:

> We entirely ready to have God remove all of these defects of character.

The person first identifies and writes down their character defects. The creator proposes an interface for recording these as part of becoming ready for change.

### Step 7: Asking for help and cultivating awareness

The creator describes acknowledging the identified character defects, turning them over to a higher power, and asking God to help remove these shortcomings.

Creator's wording:

> It's not like they ever go away, but now it's something that this allows us to become aware of our character defects, and having the awareness allows us to move through the world a little bit better.

The emphasis is on awareness and how it changes everyday life, rather than a promise that the patterns disappear permanently.

### Step 8: Listing harms and becoming willing to make amends

Creator's wording:

> Made lists of all the persons we had harmed and became willing to make amends to all of them.

UI idea from the creator: an exercise to list people one has wronged and prepare to make amends. The emphasis includes willingness; the list does not itself require immediate contact.

### Step 9: Making amends without causing further harm

Creator's wording:

> Go back and make amends to such people wherever possible, except when doing so is going to injure others.

The creator emphasizes acknowledging when contact could be detrimental. For example, contacting an ex-wife or ex-girlfriend with whom one no longer has a relationship might cause harm; in that situation, the person should not initiate contact. This is a contextual example, not a blanket rule about all former relationships.

### Step 10: Daily inventory and prompt repair

Creator's wording:

> Continue to take the personal inventory, and when we're wrong, promptly admit it.

The creator describes a daily mini inventory as ongoing maintenance. When one has acted wrongly, acknowledge it and make an appropriate amend promptly.

Everyday example from the creator: after showing up poorly in a meeting, send an email that night or the next day saying, "Hey, I'm sorry that I acted this way." This illustrates how the practice extends beyond addiction into ordinary work and relationships. The example describes an action a person might choose; it does not authorize the AI to send messages.

UI idea from the creator: a recurring daily mini inventory that supports noticing one's conduct, acknowledging wrongs, and identifying appropriate repair.

### Step 11: Daily prayer, meditation, and conscious contact

The creator interprets Step Eleven as improving conscious contact with God as each person understands Him through daily prayer and meditation. The apparent transcription "software meditation" is understood here as "sought through prayer and meditation," consistent with the original Step Eleven and the explanation that follows.

Daily meditation is a key part of the program. The creator suggests accessible durations of one, two, five, or perhaps thirty minutes, with an emphasis on daily practice. Prayer should be practiced in a way the person finds useful.

The creator describes a spiritual understanding in which people ask a question through prayer and may receive insight through meditation. This is recorded as a spiritual interpretation, not a guarantee that meditation provides an answer or evidence of divine communication.

Creator's wording:

> Not to ask for things, but ask God for guidance, ask God for strength to pursue what God's will is.

> God's will is the same as our will because God wants us to achieve what we want, but, you know, we may not predict how God wants us to achieve that.

These statements capture the creator's theology. They should not be presented by the agent as universal fact or as knowledge of God's intentions for a particular person.

UI opportunities from this explanation: support daily meditation at different durations and exercises for prayer centered on guidance and strength. Exact interactions remain to be designed.

### Step 12: Passing the practice on

The creator frames the twelve-step program as a spiritual awakening program. Following an awakening through the steps, the next practice is to pass the message or practice on to someone else.

Creator's wording:

> The next is to pass this on to somebody else. That could be part of the exercises as well.

This could become an exercise in sharing or service. The form of that exercise is not yet specified.

### Implementation questions to revisit

Before translating this framework into coaching behavior, clarify how accepting the present moment and turning things over coexist with personal agency, practical action, and seeking help. This is an open design question, not a revision to the creator's wording.

For Step Four, distinguish responsibility for one's actions and responses from responsibility for harm inflicted by someone else. Proposed coaching boundary: do not insist that someone caused or shares blame for abuse, trauma, or victimization, or require them to find wrongdoing in every resentment. This boundary is an implementation proposal, separate from the creator's interpretation.

For Step Five, decide the role of a human buddy and the AI. Any sharing feature needs explicit consent and clear control over what is shared; choosing a buddy should not automatically disclose an inventory.

For Steps Six and Seven, decide how to retain the creator's terminology while encouraging specific, changeable patterns rather than global judgments of a person's worth.

For Steps Eight through Ten, distinguish willingness, preparation, and taking action. Carry the concern about further harm into daily repair too. Proposed design: help a person consider whether contact is appropriate and prepare an amend without pressuring them or automatically contacting anyone. Specific UI fields and alternative forms of repair remain to be designed.

For Step Eleven, support reflection without claiming to interpret divine messages or endorsing every desire as God's will. Treat insights as possibilities to consider alongside practical judgment. Prayer language and meditation formats should respect the person's beliefs and preferences.

For Step Twelve, design sharing or service as voluntary and respectful of the recipient's willingness. Completing exercises should not be treated as proof that a spiritual awakening has occurred.

For each step, future notes can capture:

- The creator's wording and intended meaning.
- The spiritual principles involved.
- How it applies to addiction and to broader life struggles.
- Exercises and reflection prompts.
- Sources, differences from the AA wording, and limits on application.
