# Research prompt: Soulfire's spiritual foundation

Copy the prompt below into a research agent. If the agent cannot access this repository, attach `vision.md`, `twelve-steps.md`, and `agent-foundation.md` from this directory. Those documents contain the creator's context and distinguish it from implementation proposals.

---

You are researching the philosophical and practical foundation for Soulfire, an experimental AI spiritual companion with interactive exercises. Its creator wants to explore whether a generalized Twelve Step program can help people with addiction, compulsive patterns, and broader life struggles move toward spiritual awakening and more conscious everyday living.

This is a creative inquiry undertaken for its potential to help people. Do not organize the research around market size, monetization, positioning, or growth. Focus on meaning, intellectual honesty, practical usefulness, and respect for the people who might use it.

## Starting material

Read these repository documents or their attached copies before researching:

- `docs/context/vision.md`
- `docs/context/twelve-steps.md`
- `docs/context/agent-foundation.md`

Treat the original AA Steps, the creator's adaptation, and the assistant's implementation proposals as distinct. Do not silently replace the creator's spiritual perspective with your own. You may question, qualify, and propose alternatives, but label them clearly.

The creator's adaptation, summarized:

1. Admit powerlessness over how the present moment manifests; recognize how resistance can make life unmanageable.
2. Come to believe that spirituality and a power greater than oneself can restore sanity.
3. Turn one's will, life, conflicts, and complications over to that higher power.
4. Make a searching, fearless moral inventory of perceived wrongs and resentments; explore one's own part in resentment.
5. Admit the inventory to God, oneself, and another person. Whether AI can serve any part of this role is unresolved; a human buddy or sponsor may be essential.
6. Identify and write down character defects, becoming ready for their removal.
7. Ask the higher power for help with shortcomings. Awareness can change how one lives even when patterns do not disappear permanently.
8. List people one has harmed and become willing to make amends.
9. Make amends when appropriate, avoiding contact or action that would cause further harm.
10. Maintain a daily mini inventory; promptly acknowledge wrongs and make appropriate repair in ordinary relationships and work.
11. Practice prayer and meditation daily. Prayer emphasizes guidance and strength rather than obtaining things; meditation may invite insight. The creator believes God's will aligns with one's aspirations, though the path may be unpredictable. Preserve this as the creator's theological position, not a universal fact.
12. Share the practice with others following spiritual awakening, potentially through service or an exercise.

Daily meditation is central, with approachable durations such as one, two, five, or thirty minutes. The product should offer interactive practices, not only conversation. Addiction remains important, but the agent should explore patterns without assuming that everyone has an addiction or equating all behaviors clinically.

## Research questions

### 1. Understand the Twelve Steps and their origins

Investigate the Steps' spiritual, historical, and practical foundations. Examine Jung's encounter with Rowland H., the documented correspondence between Jung and Bill Wilson, and other influences including the Oxford Group and William James. Establish what is directly documented, what is retrospective testimony, and what is disputed. Do not assume the Steps derive entirely from Jung.

Explore established adaptations beyond AA, including relevant secular and non-theistic approaches. Explain what these preserve, change, or lose. Distinguish evidence about supported recovery programs from evidence about a generalized AI spiritual companion; benefits in one setting do not establish benefits in another.

### 2. Explore Jung in depth

Research individuation, shadow, persona, projection, complexes, the ego and Self, symbols, dreams, the numinous, and Jung's understanding of religion and spiritual transformation.

Ask how these ideas could deepen each step without forcing a correspondence. Examine tensions as well as parallels: surrender versus ego development, moral inventory versus shadow integration, spiritual ideals versus avoidance, and awakening versus inflation or claims of special spiritual status.

Clearly distinguish Jung's own writings from later Jungian interpretations and popular internet summaries. Identify concepts that require trained human facilitation or are unsuitable for automated interpretation. Avoid treating symbolic readings of dreams as diagnoses or facts.

### 3. Expand thoughtfully into related teachings

Select a small number of additional traditions or thinkers because of substantive relevance. Possible avenues include contemplative Christianity, Buddhist teachings on suffering and attachment, Stoic distinctions about control, William James on religious experience, and modern approaches to acceptance, compassion, and values-based action.

Explain both convergences and differences. Do not collapse different understandings of God, self, surrender, desire, or awakening into a single doctrine. Psychological methods are not proof of metaphysical claims, and historical philosophy is not automatically clinical evidence.

### 4. Examine the creator's generalization honestly

For each step, consider where the adaptation is illuminating and where its wording could mislead. In particular:

- How can accepting an already occurring moment coexist with agency, boundaries, planning, and action?
- How does surrender differ from resignation or avoidance of responsibility?
- How can examining one's response to resentment avoid implying that a person caused abuse or must share blame for harm done to them?
- How can moral inventory and character-defect language support accountability without reinforcing shame or a global sense of worthlessness?
- What human relational functions are important in disclosure, witnessing, sponsorship, and repair? Which should AI support rather than replace?
- When are direct amends appropriate, inappropriate, or better considered with a qualified human? Include the interests and boundaries of the recipient.
- How can prayer for guidance preserve discernment rather than treating every desire or meditative insight as God's will?
- How can sharing and service remain voluntary rather than becoming recruitment or pressure?
- What might spiritual awakening mean across traditions, and why should exercise completion not certify it?

Do not assume spirituality is necessary for everyone or that spiritual struggle explains every mental health condition. Address relevant limitations proportionately, without turning the entire inquiry into a warning document.

### 5. Investigate neuroscience, psychology, and possible mechanisms

Research whether, for whom, and under what conditions the proposed practices help, and what mechanisms might explain their effects. Investigate meditation, prayer, acceptance, perceived control, surrender, self-reflection, disclosure to a trusted person, social support, forgiveness, amends, service, and sustained daily practice. Include research on craving, compulsive behavior, and recovery where relevant.

Start with outcomes people care about: distress, rumination, emotional regulation, craving, behavior change, relationships, functioning, and well-being. Then examine proposed mechanisms such as attention, metacognition, learning, stress regulation, social connection, expectancy, meaning-making, and habit formation. Brain imaging or a biological correlate alone does not establish a causal mechanism or a useful outcome.

Look for systematic reviews, meta-analyses, well-designed controlled studies, and relevant primary mechanistic research. Report mixed findings, null results, adverse experiences, study limitations, and differences among practices and populations. Distinguish correlation from causation, laboratory findings from everyday outcomes, and short-term changes from lasting effects. Include effect sizes and uncertainty where available and interpretable; do not manufacture precision.

Assess evidence specifically for brief daily practices, including one-, two-, and five-minute meditation. Do not transfer findings from intensive retreats or longer structured programs to very short sessions without qualification. Consider adherence, dose, accessibility, and whether an active comparison condition explains apparent benefits.

Distinguish spiritual or religious practice from community participation, ritual, therapeutic support, and expectancy. Examine both spiritual and secular explanations without assuming they are interchangeable. Neuroscience cannot establish the existence of God, reveal God's intentions, or verify that a meditative insight is a divine message.

The creator mentions Andrew Huberman's discussions of God and spirituality as a research lead. Locate the actual relevant episodes, interviews, transcripts, or statements; do not assume the wording or scientific basis of any claim. Cite dates and timestamps, identify who made the claim, and trace empirical claims to the underlying studies. Separate Huberman's personal views, guest views, hypotheses, and summaries of research. Evaluate those studies independently rather than treating a public figure's authority as evidence. If the referenced discussion cannot be identified, report that uncertainty and continue the broader research.

Avoid simplistic explanations such as a single brain region causing spirituality, every rewarding behavior being a dopamine addiction, or a named neural network proving a spiritual doctrine. Describe findings at the level the evidence supports.

### 6. Translate research into exercises and companion behavior

Propose a curated exercise library that an agent could select and personalize for a person's stated struggle. Consider resentment inventories, awareness of recurring patterns, disclosure preparation, amends planning, daily reflection, prayer for guidance, short meditation, and service.

For each proposed exercise, specify:

- Purpose, related step, and supporting source or rationale.
- Appropriate situations and situations needing adaptation or human support.
- A small set of prompts and a suitable UI interaction.
- A concrete reflection or next action.
- How the user can skip, pause, or keep material private.
- What the AI should and should not infer from the responses.

Label research-supported practices, adaptations inspired by sources, and original design proposals separately. Do not describe a newly invented exercise as clinically validated. Default to minimal collection of sensitive information; do not propose automatic disclosure or outreach.

## Source and evidence standards

Use primary sources wherever possible, authoritative historical material, and credible scholarly or clinical research for empirical claims. Give stable links, full titles, authors, publication dates, and page, section, or letter references when available. Cite the sources adjacent to the claims they support.

Verify quotations against their sources. Distinguish direct quotations from paraphrases, and avoid lengthy copyrighted reproduction. Include edition or translation details where they affect meaning. If a source is unavailable, say so; never invent citations, quotations, historical details, or study findings.

Keep theological belief, philosophical interpretation, historical fact, clinical evidence, and product design proposals distinguishable. Explain uncertainty and disagreements. Verify AA text attribution and relevant publication/reuse requirements through authoritative guidance without presenting legal speculation as fact.

Do not diagnose users, promise recovery or spiritual awakening, or present the companion as a replacement for a clinician, crisis support, or a human sponsor. Consider how practices should change when a person is acutely distressed or meditation is destabilizing. Keep detailed treatment protocols outside the scope of this research.

## Deliverables

Write a substantial but usable research dossier containing:

1. A concise synthesis of the strongest findings and unresolved tensions.
2. A sourced account of the Jung–AA connection and other historical influences.
3. A twelve-step matrix: original meaning, creator's adaptation, relevant Jungian concepts, relevant additional teachings, practical implications, cautions, and citations. Mark mappings as interpretive where appropriate.
4. A proposed generalized version of all twelve steps, explicitly labeled as a draft for the creator's review. Explain substantive wording changes rather than silently making them.
5. A starter library of 8–12 concrete interactive exercises, including a sustainable daily meditation practice.
6. Companion voice and behavior recommendations, with several example responses showing compassion, accountability, spiritual openness, and respect for agency.
7. A division of responsibilities among AI, the person, a human buddy or sponsor, and qualified professionals.
8. An annotated source library organized for future retrieval, with a distinction between primary sources and commentary.
9. An evidence matrix for the proposed practices: outcome studied, population, practice and dose, comparison condition, effect and uncertainty, plausible mechanism, evidence strength with rationale, limitations, and citations. Explain where the evidence does or does not transfer to an AI-guided exercise.
10. A source-checked review of relevant Huberman discussions, separating spiritual interpretation from empirical claims and linking those claims to their underlying research.
11. Plain-language explanations of supported mechanisms that the companion could use without overstating evidence, alongside examples of tempting claims it should avoid.
12. Questions that genuinely need the creator's judgment, plus recommended next experiments. Do not make unanswered preferences block research.

If repository access is available, save the dossier under `docs/research/` as new files with a navigable index. Do not overwrite the creator's context files. Otherwise, deliver the dossier in clearly organized Markdown.

Begin with the supplied context and pursue the research independently. Seek depth and practical insight rather than collecting the largest possible number of loosely related references.
