# Additional perspectives: first integration draft

Status: conceptual draft for the creator's review. This captures perspectives, not executable agent instructions, approved exercises, or a clinical framework.

Source: [Four Perspectives on Soulfire: Jung, Maté, Ghiyam and Hudson](../research/four-perspectives-jung-mate-ghiyam-hudson.md), supplied by the creator and preserved unchanged. Its accounts of teachings, hypothetical applications, and product proposals are separate categories. Citations and quotations have not been independently verified here. None of the thinkers endorses Soulfire.

## Place in the foundation

Keep the creator's generalized Twelve Steps as the backbone, and the [step–principles map](step-principles-map.md) as the vocabulary connecting steps to spiritual principles. Add these perspectives as lenses for understanding a person's experience. They enrich the program without automatically rewriting its steps or replacing its existing map.

The creator further clarifies that the meaning of every step can continue to expand over time. Incorporate new insights within this twelve-step foundation rather than building a parallel program for each teacher. Similar terms may describe the same practice: the creator grounds surrender in Step Three, accepting the world as it is and turning things over to a higher power. Record connections and useful distinctions without requiring a new label for every interpretation. This evolving understanding is distinct from silently changing the captured source wording or treating all traditions as equivalent.

Working synthesis: understand what is happening, make room for the person's experience, explore a pattern with compassion, take responsibility where appropriate, and retain connection to spirituality and other people. The existing action-oriented strategy remains available; action need not precede listening, and listening need not become endless analysis.

## Four distinct lenses

| Lens | Contribution described in the supplied material | Possible relevance to the Steps | Limit on interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jung | Integration, persona, projection, unrealized capacities, and humility about spiritual experiences | 4, 6–7, 10–12 | Do not tell someone what their shadow is, presume projection, or interpret an experience as divine instruction |
| Gabor Maté | Compassionate curiosity about what a compulsion provides; pain, connection, authenticity, and adaptations | 1, 4, 6–7 | Do not assume every pattern originates in trauma or that understanding pain replaces practical or medical help |
| David Ghiyam | Pause before reaction; pray for strength; examine desire, sharing, and motives | 3, 9–12 | Treat this as his particular teaching, not all Kabbalah; do not infer spiritual causes of illness or promise material outcomes |
| Joe Hudson | Emotional honesty, wonder, self-understanding, and curiosity about avoidance | 4, 6–7, 10–11 | Treat the Golden Algorithm as a coaching heuristic, not a universal law or explanation of harm inflicted on someone |

These lenses should not become four impersonated mentors or personas. Soulfire can have its own coherent voice and explain a source when useful. Neither the app nor the uploaded commentary speaks for these people.

## Themes to carry forward

### Curiosity alongside responsibility

Ask what a pattern offers or protects before reducing it to something to eliminate. Understanding a pattern does not excuse harmful behavior. Responsibility does not require condemning the whole person.

Keep uncertainty explicit: a behavior may offer relief, stimulation, belonging, distraction, or something the person cannot yet name. A question should not presuppose hidden pain or a childhood cause.

### Feelings and reactions are different

Making room for anger does not require acting aggressively. Pausing a reaction does not require suppressing the feeling. This distinction may connect Hudson's welcoming, Maté's authenticity, and Ghiyam's restraint, but it is our proposed synthesis, not proof that their frameworks agree.

### Integration and change can coexist

The creator's Step Seven already emphasizes awareness rather than permanent disappearance of shortcomings. Jung's integration lens adds the possibility that a problematic expression contains a useful capacity: anger may coexist with the need for a boundary, for example. Do not assume every harmful pattern has a hidden gift or reinterpret it for the person.

Preserve traditional "defects of character" wording in the captured source. Whether the UI uses "patterns," traditional language, or a person's preference is still a design decision.

### Desire deserves reflection, not automatic validation

Preserve the creator's theology about God's will and aspiration. These perspectives introduce questions about whose interests a desire serves, whether a plan reflects a passing reaction, and how it affects others. They do not establish that the ego's wants, Jung's Self, and a higher power's will are identical.

Receiving in order to share is one lens, not a demand that every personal desire prove its usefulness to others. Rest, safety, and personal needs can matter in their own right.

### Spiritual depth includes uncertainty and ordinary life

Keep insights provisional, human relationships important, and service voluntary. A powerful experience does not certify awakening or make a person an authority over others. Avoid turning spiritual practice into performance for the AI, self-monitoring scores, or approval seeking.

## Proposed additions to our principle vocabulary

Record these as proposed additions alongside the existing map, not verified NA or AA mappings:

- Steps 1 and 4: compassionate curiosity and self-understanding.
- Step 3: choice before reaction, with room for the feeling itself.
- Steps 6–7: integration, authenticity, and self-acceptance.
- Step 10: emotional honesty and truth expressed with care.
- Step 11: wonder and reflection on desire and sharing.
- Step 12: humility and sharing without requiring recognition.

Some already overlap with existing principles. Consolidate names later rather than multiplying labels now. User language may be simpler than any teacher's terminology.

## Tensions to preserve

- **Removal versus integration:** the source traditions differ; the companion need not declare one universal model of change.
- **Surrender versus authenticity:** releasing control must not become abandoning needs or accepting coercion.
- **Emotion versus action:** neither emotional avoidance nor prolonged introspection should become the prescribed path for everyone.
- **Certainty versus discernment:** respect faith without claiming certainty about outcomes or supernatural instructions.
- **"Our part" versus harm done to us:** curiosity about responses must not imply responsibility for victimization.
- **Human witnessing versus AI:** the new document recommends keeping Step Five human; the creator's question remains open. Preserve that recommendation without recording it as a decided change.

## Scope for later work

Do not adopt the attachment's rename requests, exercise durations, automatic branching rules, or sequence of prompts as final requirements. In particular, "feeling → story → need → part → action" is a possible conversational rhythm, not a mandatory questionnaire. Making emotion-focused reflection brief does not by itself establish its safety or effectiveness.

Do not translate these lenses into unsupervised trauma processing, memory recovery, active imagination, intensive emotional release, or branded clinical methods. Later product design should decide when simple reflection is appropriate and when human support matters.

For now, retain the source, this synthesis, its provenance, and its unresolved differences. Later we can review language with the creator, verify priority sources, and decide which ideas actually improve the four prototype journeys. No new exercise implementation or strategy overhaul is implied by this capture.
