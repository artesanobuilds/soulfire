## `current_audio_finish.py` — verdicts on the asked risks

**Final-cliff risk: none in either branch, with one quantified residual.** In the padding-only branch, `end = last + 0.35 s` and the 3–4 s cos² fade terminates at `end` with gain exactly 0 (`master[-1]=0` as belt-and-braces), so there is no cut of audible material without a fade. In the terminal-drop branch (`end = hard + 0.12`), the drop instant sits 0.12 s before the end of a ≥3 s fade, where the envelope gain is ≈0.004 (≈ −24 dB) — the residual cliff is attenuated ~24 dB and ends at zero. Verified against the records: **8 of 72** candidates used the drop branch, and because it carries a +3.0 `boundaryPenalty`, **zero drop-branch pieces were selected** — the active 36 are all "low-activity terminal padding only" trims (max tail removed 9.45 s on a rejected candidate; mean 2.97 s).

**Inappropriate rest removal: interior rests are safe by construction; one residual outro risk.** The trim anchors on the *last* supra-threshold activity scanning from the end, so an interior rest followed by any music can never be cut. The drop rule additionally requires no future band activity within 22 dB of the piece's 95th-percentile level and only scans after the 72% mark. The residual risk: a deliberately quiet final section more than 22 dB below the loud sections (plausible at the measured LRA of 10–16 LU) would count as "no meaningful return" and be cut — but since no selected piece used that branch, this risk is confined to rejected candidates whose raws are preserved anyway. Two cosmetic notes: the `before` level mixes full-band dB against a band-limited reference (sub-80 Hz rumble can inflate it — only makes the drop rule *more* likely to trigger), and the in-script `interiorMaxSampleDifference` check compares the master against the same array it was sliced from, so it can only catch slicing bugs; the real interior check is in the (unrun) verify script, which correctly compares against `raw-source.wav`.

## Duration contracts: consistent, with one honest limitation

Raw is asserted within ±3 s of the request (all raws are 60.0 s); master = raw cropped+faded; bed must equal master length (asserted in the verify script — again, needs to actually run). The domain gate was properly updated (`src/domain/current-music.ts:52-56`): preparation-aware samples now require `metrics.duration` = `playableDuration` ±0.05, raw vs request ±3, and playable ≥ 0.75 × request — so trimmed pieces no longer trip the old ±3 s rule. Selection floor held: playable range 50.5–60.0 overall, **54.2–60.0 selected, 34 of 36 under 60 s**. Limitation to state plainly anywhere the catalogue is described: these are "up to 60 s" pieces (54–60 s playable), not uniform 60 s.

## Non-blocking limitations worth recording

- **Selection is purely `boundaryPenalty` (verified: 36/36 selected are the concept's minimum; penalty = tail-removed seconds + 3.0 for a drop).** It optimizes trim size, not music; a candidate with a beautiful natural decay (larger "low-activity tail") loses to a flatter one. Acknowledged in the selection file; Miguel's preview remains the only musical check, and `humanAudition: false` is correctly recorded.
- **Both candidates per concept share the same shift** (29 concepts shift 1 / 7 concepts shift 3, both candidates alike) — they differ only by seed, consistent with "seed variation is not musical quality proof," but it means the rebuild carries no new arm evidence.
- **Session seams will dip:** mastered pieces now end in a 3–4 s fade-to-silence, while the player's crossfade overlap is only 1 s (`current-music-audio.ts:121,134`). Crossfading two mastered pieces overlays the tail of a 3–4 s fade with a 1 s fade-in — a perceptible 2–3 s energy dip between segments. Not a defect of the finisher, but worth either widening the player crossfade toward the source fade length or crossfading from the pre-fade region for session mode.
- `rebuild-progress.json` shows 72/72 completed, zero failures, and the transcription set (144 files) exists; `future-generation-proof.json` and the policy schema wiring in `music-lab-runtime.ts` look correct, including retired pieces staying resolvable by ID for saved sessions.

**Bottom line:** the finishing code is sound on the three asked risks; the blockers are operational — run `verify-current-rebuild.py` (and produce whatever `late-release-audit.json` was meant to be), then `publish-current-catalogue.ts --activate`; until then the new catalogue is staged but not live, and the claimed independent QA does not yet exist on disk.
